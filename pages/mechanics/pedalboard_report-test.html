Printed Boxes Technical Report

Hugo REYMOND

ABSTRACT

These components were developed to address the challenge of assembling a male-female threaded connection between two materials with distinct mechanical properties: thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU), which provides flexibility, and polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG), which offers rigidity.

In the process of developing 3D-printed boxes that offer relevant properties such as lightness, customizability, and optimization, additional ideas naturally emerged for other applications related to hiking, travel, and long-distance itinerant journeys.

2. Toothbrush/toothpaste holder

The toothbrush/toothpaste holder is divided into three pieces as shown in Figure 1: the main part and two caps. The geometry of the main body is designed to create two separate internal chambers: one intended for the toothbrush and the other for the toothpaste.

2.1 Central part

The main part of the assembly has two chambers with openings in opposite directions. Figure 2 shows both openings thanks to a mirror.

To ensure a good fit between the main part and the cap, keeping a minimum gap of 0.2 mm (to be increased if the diameter is bigger) is recommended.

2.2 Toothbrush/toothpaste caps

The caps are printed with TPU material for two main technical reasons:

  • Protection. The TPU provides soft, non-breakable ends.
  • Closing safety. TPU flexibility creates friction and keeps the caps in place.

The support needed to build the internal thread is difficult to remove, especially with TPU 95A. In both cases, the thread must be reshaped with a soldering iron.

The caps are annotated “Tooth brush” and “Tooth paste” and use different surface patterns to help the user distinguish their contents.
